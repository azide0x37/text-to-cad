## Slant's written design limits

Slant's August 2025 Teleport guide distinguishes its **1 mm design target** for standing walls from its **0.8 mm listed minimum**. Other published service values include a **10 mm bridge**, **2 mm hole diameter**, **0.5 mm clearance between moving parts**, **2 mm feature**, and **3 mm pin**. The cited profile uses a 0.4 mm nozzle and 0.2 mm layers. These figures are useful when quoting work for that service. Keep their units and meanings intact. A 0.5 mm gap between mating surfaces is different from increasing a bore diameter by 0.5 mm. [Design rules and limits](https://www.slantpod.com/post/how-to-design-for-teleport) · [Service settings](https://www.slantpod.com/post/what-are-the-slant-3d-3d-printing-service-settings)

The upstream CAD skill's DFM rules use other published baselines. If one flags a bridge that would fall inside Slant's table, record the span, bridge direction, anchors and applicable profile. A slice and a coupon on the intended machine can resolve a prototype decision. For a service order, confirm the service's current limits and support-removal arrangements. [How Slant handles supports](https://www.slantpod.com/post/understanding-support-structures-in-print-on-demand-printing-at-slant3d)

Process settings also change over time. Slant listed 50° for its service in October 2024 and 45° in the 2025 design guide. Record the dated profile and angle convention used for the slice. Its print-quality case study also describes a group of miniature parts that passed a size check as a batch. Inspect each physical piece and slice the smallest detail at the production layer height. [Earlier settings](https://www.slantpod.com/post/optimizing-print-settings-slant-print-on-demand-app) · [Miniature-part case study](https://www.slantpod.com/post/what-is-slant-3d-s-print-quality)

## Additional design decisions and experiments

- **Make a permanent feature do the work of a support.** In a fan example a ring carries the growing blade tips and stiffens the finished part. In a flow fixture the slots are arranged so each new contour has supporting material. Apply the same idea to a louvered panel. Compare the added obstruction and mass with the finish, labor and scrap of a removable support. [Fan-blade rings](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SWOnmWCi6bY) · [Slotted flow fixture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Neop_O2jMkk&t=21159s)
- **Give a product family stable interfaces.** A change to a hub's bolt pattern or to a roller's texture need not move the shaft, bearing seats or mounting surface. Drive the variable features from the mating dimensions and check small and large variants. A generous lead-in can guide the real shaft past an edge even where the running bore needs a close fit. [Parametric wheel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=id_b-5kcIpo) · [Roller variants](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Neop_O2jMkk&t=38348s)
- **Bracket or spring.** A bracket with a long lever and narrow root can benefit from a wider section around the working envelope. For a spring, that same added material can remove the travel it needs. Section the component, mark the rigid and deflecting regions, and change their roots independently. [Wire-clip redesign](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=as84ZBjdmxU) · [Keeping snap arms usable](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_y8Yvu1FQIE)
- **Rods and bosses.** Try a longitudinal flat when an otherwise round rod needs to print on its side. Preserve bearing surfaces that require a circle. A slim longitudinal relief can induce extra contours where a pin meets a base. Compare the slicer's actual paths before claiming the pin gains strength. [Rods](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WfP-ZOnlFPM) · [Induced perimeters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lq-SoGgKOcQ) · [Horizontal pins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Iyxp_Rjz3wo)
- **Surface versus fit.** Compare text added to a face with detail cut into it. Select a pattern and build face that produce the desired contrast without crossing a mating datum. When an internal shelf pulls a visible band into the outside wall, inspect the thermal transition as well as the exterior finish. [Texture placement](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bahEMp0dTas) · [Visible enclosure surfaces](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5WdUF4Y_FI) · [Shrink lines](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d61u_lKFa-U)
- **Loading hardware after printing.** Check the insertion and escape routes of a nut or magnet and how the working load reaches its shoulder. Include a way to control magnet polarity and assembly order. Choose the closure only after checking that the real component can be installed. [Magnet retention](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BwUzOJ8B_H0) · [Nut capture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WMbmDfGEygk)
- **Match a strength claim to the experiment.** Slant compressed hollow cubes to isolate wall thickness, bent beams with varied infill, and compressed TPU specimens to explore stiffness. For a new housing, design a sample that loads the actual joint in its actual print direction. A cube's peak compressive load does not specify snap-arm fatigue or screw pullout. [Hollow-cube compression](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PQHKQH5imkI) · [Beam bending](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZNX7Mzjq_fk) · [TPU stiffness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3gxsLZITN2c)
- **Cases with functional surfaces.** Trace the hand or cable envelope around a dock, handle or pocket. For a housing that sheds water, follow the path from the outside past the seam and vents in the *installed* orientation. Verify drainage and ingress on the assembled part. [Phone dock](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QcqTncnBDAw) · [Handle alternatives](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CjDpfGzLTmY) · [Enclosure seam](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=prMUfQ9y7Rk) · [Vent examples](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bO39lWkaspA)
