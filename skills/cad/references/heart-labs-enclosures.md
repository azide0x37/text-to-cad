# Replaceable enclosure panels and floor mounts

Use Heart Labs’ Junction Box PLUS and Zip Tile as references when a printed enclosure needs interchangeable ports, serviceable electronics or a place to restrain a cable. The box has interchangeable windows and a lid secured by bayonet screws. The observations below come from sections and fit checks on the original models. [Junction Box PLUS – Full Set](https://www.printables.com/model/1223646-junction-box-plus-full-set) · [Heart Labs’ model store](https://www.printables.com/@DigitalHardwareStore/store)

## Let the closing sequence retain the panel

Seat the window in the open shell, then close the lid. The window’s flange meets shoulders in the shell and lid. The shoulders react loads normal to the panel, while closing the lid takes away the upward path used to remove it. On a new design, a broad lead-in can make the correct orientation apparent by touch. Give the user a way to see or feel that the panel has seated before the lid fastener starts pulling.

**Check the sequence in CAD.** In the original models, closed and vented windows both seated without volumetric interference in a shallow side wall and a taller end wall. Moving either window a short distance down produced interference with the base alone. Moving it up produced interference with the lid alone. Repeat those tests on the replacement, then take the lid away and sweep the *populated* panel out. A long board, plugged-in lead or tight bend can block a path the blank clears.

**Choose what stays interchangeable.** Keep the seating shoulder, flange and local corner profiles at their mating dimensions. When extending a panel, change the straight span between the ends. Vary the face inside that perimeter for a gland, vent or connector. Check each distinct shell section and compare the new perimeter against its matching stock blank. A taller version may introduce retention features that uniform scaling would distort. If someone needs to change a port while the lid stays shut, give that port its own accessible fastener or cartridge.

## Give the load a route into the shell

A Zip Tile eye offers two crosswise passages under a raised crown. Sections through a standalone tile and a Junction Box PLUS floor eye showed the same arrangement. Once aligned, their raised eye regions shared more than 99% of their union volume. A straight tie-sized gauge cleared both passages in both parts. Raising it into the crown made it collide. The separate ClickBoard model carries a board in upper guides and presents retaining surfaces to the floor mount below.

- **Cable load.** Route the tie under the crown and the cable through its loop. Check where the tie head lands, how the person threads and cuts the tail, and which supports take the pull. A loop over a smooth bundle can still let it slide lengthwise. If axial restraint matters, provide a supported stop or suitable strain relief and test a pull in that direction.
- **Board load.** Trace a plug insertion force from receptacle to PCB, through a board stop and into the wall or floor. A floor clip can support a long board. A panel-supported board can leave the eye free for wiring and come out with the window. Combining both mounts makes their installation directions and tolerance stack part of the design. Sweep the assembly with the clip deflected and again with it released.

## Choose the print pose for the modified part

A separate window can lie on a broad face while the shell prints floor-down. A custom bezel, recessed overmold or long internal rail changes the first-layer contact and where new islands appear. Slice both the stock pose and a credible alternative. At the port shoulder, follow the first roof paths to their anchors. At a latch, look for continuous perimeters into the root and a clear gap along its working length. Include the support interface in the inspection if the chosen process uses one.

For a physical fit check, print the mating corner and a length of straight flange with the intended material, orientation and first-layer settings. Seat and release it by hand. Check whether the panel rocks, needs excessive force or loosens after cycling. On the populated assembly, try the actual plug and cable pull with the lid installed. Open the lid and check that a finger can reach the release and withdraw the board.
